Fix root cause of Nisansa endpoint failures: it breaks on the sequence ඤ්

The endpoint was never rate limiting us. It fails, deterministically, on one
input: U+0DA4 (nya) followed by U+0DCA (al-lakuna). Measured directly:

  ඤ  alone            -> works
  ක් and every other consonant + al-lakuna (41 tested) -> works
  ඤ්                  -> fails, 3/3, every time
  batch minus that one word -> passes immediately

1,144 of 450,587 corpus words (0.254%) contain it. A request fails if any item
in it does, so a 250-word batch fails ~30-47% of the time - exactly the
"refusal rate" we kept measuring and misreading as load shedding. It also
explains the history: the first 25k words were an alphabetical region with no
such words and ran at ~500 words/s, and because affected words cluster
alphabetically, a shard could appear to fail from its very first request.

Retrying was provably useless: across 60 failing batches at 6 attempts each,
not one ever succeeded on a later attempt. The old escalating backoff spent up
to 38s sleeping per failure, which is why throughput was ~3 words/s instead of
the ~133 words/s measured without it.

Changes:
- filter the known-broken sequence client-side; never send it
- treat a placeholder as deterministic, never retried; retries now cover only
  genuine transport errors
- bisect a failing batch immediately with no waiting, so an uncatalogued blind
  spot costs its own request rather than its whole batch
- record unromanizable items in unsupported.json, excluded from "to do" so a
  shard can legitimately reach 100%; kept out of shard files so those stay
  pure endpoint output
- make the shard lock atomic (O_EXCL): check-then-create let two runs started
  in the same second both proceed
- status: count unromanizable items as resolved, and stop decompressing the
  manifest once per shard (6.5s -> 0.8s)

Pre-registered all 1,144 affected words.
